# Surge protection (P5): user manual, maintenance and FAQ

For the owner of the house. The surge protective devices (SPDs) take lightning and switching
surges to earth before they reach your appliances and your solar charger. They need no
attention in normal use, but they wear: each large surge uses up a little of them, and at the
end of its life an SPD disconnects itself and shows it.

> The SPDs sit in the main board, the sub-board, the PV junction box on the roof and beside the
> solar charger. Only a qualified electrician opens these enclosures.

## Where they are and what they show

| Where | What you see | Healthy | Needs replacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main board | flag in the window of the type 1+2 SPD (DEHNshield); a lamp if your electrician wired its contact | green; lamp on | red; lamp off |
| Sub-board | window on the type 2 SPD; a lamp if wired | green; lamp on | red; lamp off |
| Small enclosure at the outlets for sensitive equipment | window on the type 3 SPD (DEHNrail) | green | red |
| PV junction box (roof) | the **lamp beside the solar charger** | on | **off** |
| Beside the solar charger | window on the PV SPD | green | red |

The two coils in the solar charger's PV enclosure (decoupling chokes) are part of the
protection: they get warm in full sun (about 8 W each), which is normal. Do not remove them.

## Maintenance

- **Once a year** and **after every thunderstorm with a strike nearby:** look at the windows
  and the lamp.
- **Red window, red flag or dark lamp:** call the electrician. The protection at that place is
  gone until it is replaced: the plug-in module of the type 2, type 3 and PV SPDs (the base
  stays), the whole device for the type 1+2 (DEHNshield, which is not pluggable). The
  installation keeps working meanwhile, unprotected.
- **At the intervals your local rules set for periodic inspection:** the electrician repeats the checks of the
  commissioning procedure (earthing, insulation).
- Keep the record sheet of the commissioning with this manual.

## FAQ

**The lamp beside the charger is off, but the window on the PV SPD beside the charger is green.**
The lamp reports the SPD on the roof. Either that SPD has disconnected, or its signal cable is
broken; both need the electrician. The charger keeps charging.

**Does the protection stop a direct lightning strike on the house?**
No. It protects the electrical installation against surges on the lines and induced surges. A
direct strike on the building needs an external lightning protection system, which this
installation does not include.

**Can I plug my computer into any socket?**
The outlets behind the type 3 SPD have the best protection; use them for sensitive equipment
(computers, TV, router). The other sockets are protected by the type 2 SPD in the sub-board,
which is enough for ordinary appliances.

**Does the protection work at night, when the solar charger is off?**
Yes. It was checked with the charger running and stopped, and with the surge coming in at the
roof or at the charger's end of the cable.

**Why two SPDs on the PV side?**
The cable from the roof is 25 m long. A surge induced in it is taken to earth at the roof, before
it reaches the charger; the SPD at the charger protects against surges entering at that end.
The rules for PV installations ask for both when the cable is longer than 10 m.

**Can the SPDs cause a fire?**
Each SPD has a disconnector that takes it off the circuit when it overheats at the end of its
life (on the PV side the SCI device, which also quenches the DC arc). That is why a red window
must be acted on: the protection is off, not the risk.
