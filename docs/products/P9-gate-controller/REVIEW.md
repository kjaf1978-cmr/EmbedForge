# P9 gate controller: independent review and resolutions

Review of 18 November 2026, by an independent reviewer who had the circuit
(`app/ui/src/eda/gate.ts`), its tests, the firmware (`firmware/gate-controller/`), the routed board,
its parts list and these documents, but not the designer's conclusions. 1 blocker, 8 major and 6
minor findings, and a list of what was missing (several minor points grouped). Designators: those
of the schematic after the fixes (the MOSFET is now Q9, the shunt R28, the cut-off's divider R29 /
R33).

| # | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blocker | The shunt's part code in the parts list was a 1 mΩ part (Isabellenhütte "R001") for a 10 mΩ shunt: fitted as ordered, the cut-off would act at about 70 A and 250 A, which is no protection. Every shunt of 10 mΩ or less got the same code from the catalogue | The catalogue names the value instead of one code ("four-terminal shunt 10 mΩ … the code for 10 mΩ"); assembly step 2.1 and commissioning 1.4 check the value. The same entry was wrong on the grinder, inverter, MPPT and trimmer boards (not yet signed off): corrected there too. None of the signed-off products used it |
| 2 | Major | The 150 W (6.25 A) supply could limit a stalled gate's current below the 7 A cut-off: the gate would keep pressing until the 60 s limit | A supply of at least 10 A that delivers the 12 A stall current for 0.5 s without folding back (installation guide, technical data); DFMEA GC-12; type test TT-9 with the intended supply |
| 3 | Major | At the third trip the firmware locked out before moving back: a person trapped by the gate stayed pressed (the braked, self-locking drive keeps the force) | The third trip moves back for 0.5 s first, then locks out; a trip during that release stops and locks out. Host tests for both |
| 4 | Major | The power-up trip (10 nF from the 5 V) only worked for a step: a real supply rising over milliseconds gave a few µA, too little to trip; a watchdog reset does not touch the 5 V, so "tripped after any reset" was false | A power-on reset with a second LM393 and an RC (470k, 100 nF): it trips the cut-off for any 5 V rise faster than about 0.25 s. Simulated with a 100 ms supply rise. The claim corrected: at a watchdog reset the drivers' pull-downs stop the motor (the cut-off is not tripped) |
| 5 | Major | "Works whatever the firmware does" was false: CLR held high disabled the latch; RUN pulsed restarts the blanking each time; a shorted MOSFET left no hardware current protection | CLR through 100 nF (its rising edge only): simulated with CLR stuck high, an obstacle still trips and stays tripped. A trip drops both relays (BAT54 clamps on their drivers): simulated with the MOSFET shorted. RUN pulsing remains a firmware fault, excluded by the host tests (DFMEA GC-10). The claim rewritten in every document |
| 6 | Major | The blanking was about 0.5 s (the reviewer's estimate 0.75 s): a gate blocked from the start was not stopped for about 0.85 s, beyond EN 12453's 0.75 s; "7 A for 0.1 s" was the filter's time constant, not its trip time; the release move lay inside the blanking | Simulated: the start current is below 7 A after 65 ms. The blanking is now about 0.1 s (1 µF); a stall from the start trips 0.24 s after it (simulated, was 0.82 s); a normal start peaks at 38 % of the threshold. The filter (100k, 1 µF film) and the trip times stated: 0.1 s at 12 A, 0.27 s at 7.5 A. The 0.5 s release is watched after its first 0.1 s |
| 7 | Major | Commissioning made the first powered move without checking the direction: with the motor wires swapped, the open relay drives the gate closed, the open limit is at the wrong end and the beam does not act | Commissioning section 3: the direction checked with the gate uncoupled, the open limit and STOP stopping the motor, before the gate is coupled. The installation guide warns |
| 8 | Major | The beam was never tested (a bridged contact or a stuck receiver went unseen); the force limit is one unmonitored channel; the required performance level (EN 12453 with EN ISO 13849-1) was not mentioned | The photo-beam's transmitter is fed from the board (X14, switched by D8): before each close the firmware turns it off and requires the beam reported broken, then made again; else no close and the LED flashes (host tests; commissioning 5.3; TT-10; DFMEA GC-13). The installation guide and the user manual state that the performance levels have not been assessed; the cut-off's periodic test is not fitted (noted in GC-1) |
| 9 | Major | "The relays switch without current" was not true at the ends: the limit switch (or the beam) drops the relay under the running current | The claims corrected (DFMEA GC-6, the firmware's header): the relays break 2.6 A at the ends, STOP and the beam, the TVS taking the energy; TT-6 runs every cycle to a limit and checks the 832A's 28 V DC and NC ratings |
| 10 | Minor | The close chain (A1) and the close limit (A2) debounced separately: the close limit could read as a broken beam for a millisecond and reopen the gate at its end | The three inputs read and debounced together as one word |
| 11 | Minor | After a power cut the next pairing overwrote the newest remote; no way to erase a lost one; no pairing feedback; the LED described in the manual was never driven | Each slot keeps a sequence number (the oldest replaced, host test across a power cut); LEARN held 10 s erases; the Nano's LED (D13) shows running, pairing, erasing, lockout, a failed beam test and STOP |
| 12 | Minor | The watchdog not stopped at start-up; with the old Nano bootloader a watchdog reset loops forever | `MCUSR` cleared and the watchdog stopped in `.init3`; the Optiboot bootloader required (platformio.ini, the firmware's README, the assembly guide, TT-3) |
| 13 | Minor | Documents: EN 12453's static force wrong; GC-10 cited but missing; REVIEW.md missing; "the first press opens" not true at the open end; the release's next press not stated; the test bench's command order not the firmware's | Corrected (static force at most 150 N up to 5 s and 25 N after; GC-10 … GC-13 written; this record; the open-end case stated; the next press after a release moves away from the obstacle; the bench now clears at 150 ms, the relay at 170 ms, RUN at 200 ms as the firmware does) |
| 14 | Minor | Ratings: the 832A's 30 A is for 14 V DC; the shunt's sense pads; the LM393's offset (6.1 … 7.9 A); a 10 µF film capacitor | The relay ratings checked in TT-6; the threshold's tolerance stated and type test TT-11; the filter now 100k and 1 µF film. **Not fixed: the shunt's sense.** The four-terminal footprint's sense pads (3, 4) are on the same nets as its current pads (1, 2), so where the comparators' tracks join is the router's choice: copper shared with the motor current (about 1 mΩ would be 10 % of R28) lowers the trip current. Measured on built boards in TT-11; separate sense nets need a four-terminal shunt symbol in the tool, an open action for the tool (DFMEA section 7) |
| 15 | Missing | No status or fault indication; no STOP input; no key-switch input; the close limit, an obstacle while opening, the release and the blanking not simulated; the schematic PDF and the package paths not there yet | A red CUT-OFF LED and the Nano's LED; a STOP input (NC, in the limit switches' feed: both relays drop in hardware); a push-button / key-switch input (A3); simulations of the close limit, STOP, a stall from the start, CLR stuck, the MOSFET shorted and the beam test output (the opening obstacle's release is a firmware sequence: host tests); the package carries the schematic and the paths |
| — | Process | The simulation run ended with "Timeout calling onTaskUpdate" (exit code 1) though every test passed; `make test` failed with an absolute build path | The test file yields to vitest between simulations; the Makefile runs the tests by their path |

Verified after the fixes: `tests/gate.test.ts`, 14 of 14 (the result in the package's
`7-simulation-tests/`), and the firmware's host tests, 19 tests and 97 checks (`9-firmware/`).
